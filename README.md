**What business problem are we solving with AI, and how will we measure success?**

This question quickly reveals whether an AI initiative is connected to a real business need or simply follows a technology trend. A clear answer should identify the problem, the people affected, and the outcome the organization expects to improve. ## Why This Question Matters

AI strategy is not just about choosing tools or launching experiments. It means deciding where AI can create value, how it will fit into existing operations, and how results will be measured. Without a clear business objective, projects can consume time and budget without delivering meaningful improvements.

## Five Things to Check

- **Business value:** Is the problem specific and important?
- **Data readiness:** Is the required data reliable and accessible?
- **Technical fit:** Can the solution work with current systems?
- **Ownership:** Who is responsible for results and ongoing performance?
- **Measurement:** What metrics will demonstrate success?

## A Practical Starting Plan

1. Define the business problem and the people it affects.
2. Set measurable goals before development begins.
3. Review data quality, integration needs, and potential risks.
4. Test one focused use case with clear success criteria.
5. Evaluate the results before deciding whether to scale.

## Common Warning Signs

A weak AI strategy may involve unclear goals, missing data, no accountable business owner, or success measures that are decided only after a project launches. These gaps should be addressed before expanding investment.

## Conclusion

A strong AI strategy connects technology to a measurable business outcome. Leaders should be able to explain what problem they are solving, why AI is appropriate, and how they will know whether it works. Answering this question early helps organizations prioritize the right initiatives and make more confident investment decisions.
